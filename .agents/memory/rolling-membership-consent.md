---
name: Rolling membership commencement and consent
description: Why an agreed rolling start date survives payment delays, and why legacy anniversaries require evidence.
---

The agreed commencement date is part of the purchased commitment. Preparing its quote does not activate membership; verification changes payment/activation state, not the agreed start, duration, or anniversary. Show that date before payment.

**Why:** Re-anchoring at a delayed callback changes the contract and can extend collections beyond the term. Conversely, presenting a pending quote as active grants access before the configured activation rule is met.

**How to apply:** Preserve the consent-time start across retries and provider delays; keep early terms scheduled. The Pricing Billing Period governs duration, while monthly collection is a separate payment frequency. Select a successor in the same purchased scope at its saved renewal boundary.

Legacy dates are recoverable only from corroborated original agreement/payment/configuration evidence, with matching ownership and financials; an account creation date or year label is not commencement evidence.

**Why:** Historical rolling records can have several possible anniversaries, and silently picking one can produce an unintended charge or reminder.

**How to apply:** Keep ambiguous records review-only, and recover trustworthy records atomically without changing provider schedules.
