        "content": "<p>Founded over 400 years ago, the University of Edinburgh is a large, research-intensive institution with a strong international profile – famous, amongst other things, for the cloning of Dolly the sheep.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Meeting the demands of modern and diverse student populations</strong></p><p>Universities today face a growing challenge: how to deliver impactful careers support to increasingly diverse student populations while making the best use of limited resources. At the University of Edinburgh, this challenge is being met with a combination of data-informed strategies, targeted interventions, and innovative programmes designed to empower students at every stage of their journey.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>The scalability challenge</strong></p><p>Careers services are essential, but for many students, they rarely feel urgent. This creates a gap in engagement, particularly at critical early stages such as pre-arrival and induction. To address this, Edinburgh is embedding careers conversations early on through its Prearrival and Induction Team, ensuring students understand the long-term value of planning for success. While it doesn’t stop the last-minute rush around graduation, it does help to stem it.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Data-informed delivery</strong></p><p>One of the most effective ways to allocate resources is through evidence-based targeting. Edinburgh uses multiple data sets, including the Graduate Outcomes survey, to identify schools where students may need additional support.&nbsp;Sharon Maguire, Assistant Director of Careers Service, ensures that a baseline offer is provided to all, but schools with lower performance receive enhanced interventions, including localised one-to one support and tailored workshops.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Flagship initiatives</strong></p><ul><li><strong>Insights Programme</strong>: Delivered in partnership with Development Alumni, Insights connects students from a widening participation background with alumn<s>i</s> across the UK and internationally. Through workplace visits and networking, students gain confidence, develop skills, and build valuable connections. Over 2,000 participants have benefited so far, with success stories including students who later return as alumni hosts.</li><li><strong>Skills for Success Framework</strong>: This initiative helps embed, surface and articulate the skills that students are developing as part of their journey at the University of Edinburgh. It is a key element of the university’s learning and teaching strategy and implementation is supported by Lorna Devlin, who is seconded from an academic school.</li><li><strong>Graduation and beyond</strong>: Support doesn’t end at graduation. Edinburgh offers follow-up coaching and maintains contact for up to two years post-graduation, recognising that success looks different for everyone—whether entering the workforce immediately or returning for further study. This includes students who may study for a Masters or other postgrad qualification elsewhere, but return to Edinburgh for careers support once that’s completed.</li><li><br></li></ul><p><strong>International student engagement</strong></p><p>International students make up 40% of Edinburgh’s student base, with Chinese students accounting for 22%. These students are highly proactive in using careers services, engaging in careers fairs and workshops such as ‘Building experience’ and ‘Top tips for Assessment Centres”.&nbsp;Many are keen to also take up paid positions with the careers service, such as the role of Careers Service Assistant, where they will staff exhibition stands during events like Scottish Careers Week. Their enthusiasm highlights the importance of culturally responsive careers support.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>External pressures and opportunities</strong></p><p>Negative headlines and regional labour market variations can deter students, but Edinburgh counters this with positive narratives and alumni success stories, focusing on students’ specific circumstances. Financial pressures and sector-wide challenges underscore the need for efficient, scalable solutions.</p><p><br></p><p><strong>Looking ahead</strong></p><p>The future of careers support lies in personalisation, early engagement, and leveraging alumni networks. By combining data-driven insights with human connection, Edinburgh is creating a model that not only prepares students for the workplace but also fosters resilience and adaptability in an uncertain world.</p>",
