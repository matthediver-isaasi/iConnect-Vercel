Create a new introductory section for the existing BNMS Patients and Carers page.

## Purpose

The section should introduce nuclear medicine to patients, parents and carers who may be unfamiliar with it or anxious about an upcoming appointment.

Explain in clear, accessible language that nuclear medicine can help healthcare professionals understand how parts of the body are functioning and may also be used in some treatments.

The section should help the visitor feel informed and supported without making promises, minimising legitimate concerns or giving individual medical advice.

## Reference design

Use the supplied reference screenshots and complete Design DNA as the authoritative visual style for this section.

Use the screenshots as direct evidence of:

* Graphic and illustration treatment
* Card construction
* Visual relationships
* Composition
* Scale
* Image placement
* Section rhythm
* Responsive behaviour

Use the structured Design DNA for:

* Measured typography
* Colours
* Spacing
* Border radiuses
* Borders
* Shadows
* Button treatments
* Component recipes

The new section must clearly belong to the same design family as the reference page.

Create an original composition for this content. Do not reproduce the exact layout, section order, wording or imagery of the reference page.

Preserve the existing BNMS branding and accessibility requirements.

## Content requirements

Include:

* A concise and welcoming section heading
* A short explanation of what nuclear medicine can help clinicians understand
* A clear distinction between diagnostic uses and treatment uses, without going into procedure-specific detail
* A visual concept that helps explain nuclear medicine to a non-clinical audience
* A clear onward action inviting visitors to learn what happens during a nuclear medicine appointment

Keep the amount of text appropriate for a visually led introductory section.

Use general language and make clear that individual procedures, instructions and experiences vary.

Do not invent:

* Preparation instructions
* Appointment durations
* Radiation doses
* Clinical outcomes
* Treatment eligibility
* Procedure-specific details

Do not provide individual medical advice.

## Composition requirements

Create a deliberately designed visual composition rather than a standard heading, paragraph and row of cards.

Use the reference design language to determine the best arrangement. The section may include:

* A prominent original illustration
* Connected or offset content cards
* Varied card sizes
* Layered elements
* Supporting labels
* Visual annotations
* Decorative background forms
* A clear visual journey
* Asymmetric placement

Avoid defaulting to:

* Three identical cards in a row
* Equal-width feature boxes
* Repetitive rectangular panels
* Generic icons
* A conventional corporate feature grid
* Stock-style medical photography
* Large blocks of uninterrupted text

All essential wording must remain real accessible HTML text and must not be embedded only within an image.

## Existing-page integration

Review the Canvas content immediately before and after the insertion point.

The section must:

* Complement the existing page without repeating its title or introduction
* Create a natural transition from the preceding content
* Lead naturally towards the content that follows
* Preserve all existing manual Canvas elements
* Use the available width correctly
* Expand the parent Canvas page safely
* Avoid overlapping surrounding elements

## Responsive behaviour

Create purposefully composed desktop, tablet and mobile versions.

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

For smaller screens:

* Recompose layered elements where necessary
* Stack content in a logical order
* Preserve the visual story
* Simplify decorative elements where appropriate
* Maintain readable typography
* Keep the onward action prominent
* Preserve a logical semantic reading order

## Final review

Before presenting the result:

* Compare it against the supplied reference screenshots
* Confirm that it belongs to the same visual design family
* Confirm that the composition is original
* Check that card, illustration and button treatments are consistent with the Design DNA
* Check desktop, tablet and mobile for collisions, clipping and weak hierarchy
* Confirm that no unsupported medical claims have been introduced
* Confirm that all important content remains accessible text
